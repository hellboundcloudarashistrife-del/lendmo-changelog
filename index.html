Bringing repayments in line with Eswatini’s Consumer Credit Act, and fixes found while testing the staff and client screens. 12 changes.

Repayments How payments are applied to a loan

  1. Payments pay interest first, then the loan, then late fees

    Done

    BeforeEach payment paid off any late fee first, then interest, then the amount borrowed, one instalment at a time. The loan agreement said the same.

    NowEach payment pays the interest that’s due first, then the amount borrowed that’s due, and only then any late fees. Anything left over goes towards the next instalment. The loan agreement now says this. Agreements already signed keep their wording, but their payments follow the new order too.

    Why: Eswatini’s Consumer Credit Act (section 98) sets this order, and it means a client’s money clears what they borrowed before it goes to fees.

Collections & support Staff screens

  1. “Process dunning” explains what it did

    Done

    BeforePressing “Create in-app notices” only showed “0 in-app notices created. 0 still require processing.”, so it looked as if the button did nothing or something else had to be done first.

    NowThe page explains that the button sends a reminder to each client with a repayment due or overdue, and that this already happens automatically every 15 minutes. When there is nothing to send, it says so in plain words.

    Why: Nothing was wrong, but the page didn’t say that no client had a repayment due yet.

  2. “Dunning rules” shows when it is view only

    Done

    BeforeDirectors and other staff saw boxes that looked editable but couldn’t be clicked. The only explanation was a small line at the bottom.

    NowA “View only” note at the top says only the Owner can change this rule and nothing needs to be done first. The locked boxes are greyed out.

    Why: It looked like a fault when it is a deliberate limit on who can change a company-wide rule.

  3. “Support & hardship” is now “Customer support”

    Done

    BeforeThe menu said “Support & hardship”, and clients could choose “Repayment hardship” as a request type.

    NowThe menu and page say “Customer support”. The request types are Support question, Complaint, Difficulty repaying and Disputed transfer. Existing cases are kept as they were.

    Why: “Hardship” is not how people here describe struggling to repay.

Employment Step 3 of the application

  1. Any expense can be typed in

    Done

    BeforeEach monthly expense had to be one of LendMo’s four types. There was no way to say what an unlisted expense, such as school fees, actually was.

    NowChoosing “Other (type what it is)” opens a box to type the expense, for example school fees or transport. Staff see it next to the amount when they check affordability. Expenses saved earlier under a type that’s no longer listed keep their name.

    Why: Clients have regular costs the list doesn’t cover, and staff need to know what “other” means.

Branch managers Staff menu

  1. Branches removed from branch managers’ menu

    Done

    BeforeBranch managers had a “Branches” page in their menu, which only ever showed their own branch.

    NowBranch managers no longer see “Branches”. They still see their team under Employees. Head office roles keep the Branches page.

    Why: Each branch has exactly one manager and nobody manages more than one, so the page had nothing for them.

Screening checks and signing From LendMo’s answers

  1. Contact check is optional

    Done

    BeforeEvery loan needed a Contact check (calling the client), and even the Owner couldn’t switch it off.

    NowOnly Identity and Wallet are needed for every loan. Contact is optional, and the Owner can still make it required in Lending settings.

    Why: LendMo doesn’t phone clients. They come into the office to sign every loan.

  2. Employment check for first loans and new employers

    Done

    BeforeThe Employment check was always optional.

    NowIt’s required on a client’s first loan, or when their employer is different from their last loan. The Screening step says why (“client’s first loan” or “employer changed since their last loan”). Returning clients with the same employer can skip it.

    Why: LendMo calls the employer in exactly these cases.

  3. Fraud review shows as required when there are warning signs

    Done

    BeforeFraud review was labelled “Optional”, but approval was blocked until it was done whenever the app found a warning sign. Returning clients were also flagged because of their own earlier, paid-out loan.

    NowWhen the app finds a warning sign, such as the same MoMo number under two ID numbers, Fraud review says “Required for this application · warning signs found” and shows the warning, in the list and in its window. A returning client’s finished loans no longer count as a warning sign (loans still running are still checked).

    Why: staff couldn’t see why approval was blocked. LendMo only checks new clients across branches, and the app now does this across all branches automatically.

  4. Every loan is signed at the branch

    Done

    BeforeA client could accept their loan offer online with one click.

    NowThe client sees their offer online with “Visit your branch to sign. Bring your ID.” They can still decline online. At the branch, staff upload the signed copy and record the signing, which the payout needs. The offer message says so too. The Owner can turn this off in Lending settings.

    Why: LendMo’s clients sign for every loan in person, so staff can’t accept on their behalf.

Correcting an application When staff ask for more information

  1. Shows exactly what to fix, all at once

    Done

    BeforePressing “Resubmit for screening” could just say “Please correct the highlighted application details. [400]”, with nothing highlighted nearby. Problems came up one at a time, and older applications still asked for a term in months.

    NowA box above the button lists every problem at once, for example “Contract end date: enter the date the current contract ends” and “Documents: upload Payslip (month 2), Payslip (month 3)”. “Show me” jumps to the box, which is outlined in red. The term box is gone for one-payment loans, and older applications switch to one payment automatically. The loan amount shows its limits (E100 to E10,000).

    Why: clients and staff couldn’t tell what needed fixing.

  2. Resubmitting keeps the checks nothing changed

    Done

    BeforeEvery time a client resubmitted corrections, all screening checks and the affordability assessment were wiped, even ones about details nobody touched. Staff had to record them all again.

    NowEach check stays unless the details or documents it confirmed changed. For example, a new phone number clears only the Contact check, and a new ID card clears Identity. The affordability assessment also stays if nothing it used changed. The Screening step says which checks need recording again, and newly uploaded documents still need reviewing as before.

    Why: staff were redoing work for things that hadn’t changed.

Smaller fixes

  • Signing in as a client no longer records a hidden “staff account required” error in the browser.

Improvements to the loan application from the 27 October review, and changes so the app follows LendMo’s own affordability form and answers. 41 changes.

The whole application form

  1. Required fields are clearly marked

    Done

    BeforeNothing showed which fields had to be filled in.

    NowA red * marks every required field and document, with a note at the top explaining it. Optional fields say “(optional)”.

    Why: people couldn’t tell what was compulsory.

  2. Money amounts are easier to type

    Done

    BeforePlain numbers, like 12500.5.

    NowCommas are added as you type, like 12,500.50, and SZL is shown in front.

    Why: big amounts are easier to read and check.

Employment Step 3 of the application

  1. “Variable” removed from how often you’re paid

    Done

    BeforeMonthly, Weekly, Every two weeks or Variable.

    NowMonthly, Weekly or Every two weeks.

    Why: a pay frequency can’t be “variable”.

  2. Pay is asked for once, as gross and net

    Done

    BeforeAn unclear “monthly income after tax” box here, then the same pay asked again on the payslip.

    NowGross pay (before deductions) and net pay (take-home) are entered once, on each payslip in the next step.

    Why: the old question was confusing and asked twice.

  3. Monthly expenses are listed one by one

    Done

    BeforeOne box for the total of all expenses.

    NowPick each expense from a list (Rentals, Groceries, Utilities, Other personal expenditure) and enter the amount. The total adds up by itself.

    Why: matches LendMo’s affordability form and makes expenses easy to check.

  4. Stop orders are listed one by one

    Done

    BeforeOne box for “existing monthly debt repayments”.

    NowUp to six stop orders (amounts already taken off the salary each month), each with who it’s paid to and the amount, plus a total. Left empty if there are none.

    Why: LendMo’s affordability form lists stop orders separately.

Income & documents Step 4 of the application

  1. Three payslips, uploaded separately

    Done

    BeforeOne payslip.

    NowSeparate sections for the last three months (most recent first), each with its own gross pay, net pay and file. All three are needed.

    Why: three months of payslips are required, one per upload to avoid mix-ups.

  2. Pay date is picked from a list

    Done

    BeforeType the day of the month you’re paid.

    NowChoose from the 1st to the 31st of the month. Asked once for all three payslips.

    Why: a list removes confusion about what to type.

  3. +268 is filled in on the mobile money number

    Done

    BeforePeople had to type +268 themselves.

    Now+268 is already shown. People only type their 8 digits, e.g. 76 123 456.

    Why: typing the country code confused people.

  4. “Real net salary” is shown

    Done

    BeforeNot shown anywhere.

    NowOnce net pay is entered, the form shows what’s left each month, worked out the way LendMo’s form does it:

    • net salary
    • less total personal expenditure
    • less total stop orders
    It’s shown again on the final check page.

    Why: this is how LendMo judges whether a loan is affordable.

Loan amount & terms Step 5 of the application

  1. Sliders, with repayments shown as you slide

    Done

    BeforeType an amount and a number of months. No repayments were shown.

    NowSlide (or type) the amount and the months. The page shows straight away what you’ll repay, the interest, and the repayment dates.

    Why: people should see what a loan will cost before they apply.

  2. Costs laid out like LendMo’s “Total cost of credit”

    Done

    BeforeAmount borrowed, interest, total to repay.

    NowLoan amount, interest, total cost of credit and total repayable, as on LendMo’s form. For example, E1,200 borrowed over one month shows E300 interest and E1,500 to repay.

    Why: customers see the cost in the same format LendMo already uses.

  3. Fees mentioned up front

    Done

    Before“Fees and insurance are excluded from this estimate.”

    NowThe cost breakdown has a Fees line, which reads “None” because LendMo charges nothing besides interest. The terms say so too.

    Why: customers should know about fees before applying.

  4. LendMo’s own declaration is used

    Done

    Before“I have read these application disclosures and confirm my information is accurate.”

    NowThe customer ticks LendMo’s declaration, word for word:

    I, herewith declare/confirm that I fully understand the breakdown of the total cost of credit, and that the information I have provided for my assessment is true and correct.

    Why: it’s the wording LendMo already has clients sign.

After applying My loans and Documents

  1. A clear “thank you” when you apply

    Done

    BeforeA small line of text: “Application … has been saved.”

    NowA pop-up with a tick: “Thank you — your application has been submitted”, the reference number and what happens next. It then takes the customer to their application.

    Why: customers weren’t sure their application had gone through.

  2. One application at a time

    Done

    BeforeA customer could apply again straight away, even with an application or loan still open.

    NowA new application can only be made once the current one is declined or the loan is paid off. The form explains why. This also covers walk-in applications for the same ID number.

    Why: a customer shouldn’t have two loans going at once.

  3. Documents can’t be changed after applying

    Done

    BeforeCustomers could swap any document while their application was being reviewed.

    NowDocuments can be viewed and downloaded only. A document can be replaced only if the review team rejects it or asks for more information.

    Why: documents shouldn’t change while they’re being checked.

Staff screens

  1. Affordability check matches LendMo’s form

    Done

    BeforeBoxes for net income, essential expenses and debt repayments.

    NowNet salary, total personal expenditure and total stop orders, with real net salary worked out as staff type, next to what the customer declared. Staff also review all three payslips.

    Why: staff use the same terms and sums as LendMo’s paper form.

  2. Button text is easy to read

    Done

    BeforeSome main buttons, like “Review and save access” on a staff member’s page, had dark text on a dark blue button and were hard to read.

    NowThe text is white on the blue button. When a button can’t be used yet, it shows dark text on a light background instead of fading out.

    Why: the button labels couldn’t be read.

  3. Staff access settings fit the page

    Done

    BeforeOn a staff member’s page, the tick boxes for their duties were stretched across the whole column. Their labels were pushed off the edge, the page scrolled sideways, and the rows were spaced unevenly.

    NowThe tick boxes are normal size and the duties line up in two neat columns (one column on a phone). Nothing runs off the page.

    Why: the settings didn’t fit or line up, even though there isn’t much on the page.

  4. Screening checks explain themselves

    Done

    BeforeA list of checks marked “Pending · Not recorded”, with no way to tell which were needed, what each meant, or how to complete them.

    NowEach check says what staff should do (for example, Contact: call the applicant to confirm their number) and whether it’s required for approval or optional. Each one has its own “Record this check” button.

    Why: staff got stuck on the screening step.

  5. Wallet check no longer needs a file

    Done

    BeforeThe wallet ownership check couldn’t be passed without a wallet ownership file, even though customers are told that file is optional.

    NowStaff can pass the wallet check by recording how they confirmed the registered name (for example, a MoMo name lookup). A file can still be attached if there is one.

    Why: applications without the optional file could never be approved.

  6. Recording a check is quicker and harder to get wrong

    Done

    BeforeOne long form at the bottom of the page with seven boxes, including two date-and-time pickers and a reference number. The result started on “Pending”, so a check could be saved as not passed without anyone noticing.

    Now“Record this check” opens a small window for that check. You pick Passed, Failed or Couldn’t check yet (nothing is picked for you), choose how you checked from a list, and write what you found. The time is filled in and the check lasts as long as the company setting says. A reference number is only needed for a credit bureau check.

    Why: an ID check was saved as “Pending” by mistake.

  7. Screening shows what to do, in order

    Done

    BeforeNothing said that documents must be verified before the checks, or that the affordability assessment has to be run last.

    NowThree numbered steps sit at the top: verify the documents, record the required checks (for example “1 of 3 passed”), then run the affordability assessment. Each step gets a tick when it’s done. If a check needs a document that hasn’t been verified yet, the window says so and has a button that opens Documents.

    Why: the ID check couldn’t pass because the ID card hadn’t been verified, and the screen didn’t say so.

  8. Reviewing a document no longer wipes other checks

    Done

    BeforeMarking any document as verified or rejected erased every check already recorded on the application.

    NowOnly checks that used that document are cleared. For example, rejecting the ID card clears the Identity check, but the Contact check stays.

    Why: finished checks were disappearing.

  9. All three payslips count as evidence

    Done

    BeforeOnly the month 1 payslip could back up the employment check and the affordability assessment.

    NowAny of the three payslips can be used.

    Why: the form now collects three payslips.

Loan product and lists From LendMo’s answers

  1. Loans match LendMo’s product

    Done

    BeforeTest values: SZL 500 to 50,000, repaid over 1 to 24 months, with 25% interest added every month.

    NowE100 to E10,000, repaid in one payment, with 25% interest charged once. The “how many months” slider is gone. For example, E2,000 borrowed means E500 interest and E2,500 to repay.

    Why: LendMo’s loans are once-off payday loans at 25%.

  2. No fees besides interest

    Done

    BeforeA test late fee of SZL 100 on overdue payments.

    NowNo late fee. The cost breakdown shows Fees: None, and the loan agreement says there is no late payment fee.

    Why: LendMo charges no fees, only interest.

  3. MTN Mobile Money only

    Done

    BeforeCustomers could choose MTN MoMo or eMali.

    NowMTN MoMo is the only choice.

    Why: LendMo only uses MTN Mobile Money.

  4. Every bank is in the list

    Done

    BeforeFNB, Swazi Bank, Building Society, Nedbank and Other. Standard Bank was missing and Swazi Bank used its old name.

    NowEswatini Bank (formerly Swazi Bank), FNB, Nedbank, Standard Bank, Swaziland Building Society (SBS) and Other. Saved applications using the old names are updated automatically.

    Why: LendMo accepts clients from all banks.

  5. Dependants question removed

    Done

    BeforeThe form asked for the number of dependants, but nothing used the answer.

    NowThe question is gone from the form and the staff screens.

    Why: LendMo doesn’t use dependants when deciding a loan.

  6. Bank statement asks for 3 months

    Done

    Before“Upload a recent bank statement.”

    Now“Upload your bank statements for the last 3 months. One file covering all three months is fine.”

    Why: LendMo needs 3 months of statements on a client’s first visit.

  7. LendMo’s own branches

    Done

    BeforeMade-up test branches such as Manzini, Nhlangano, Siteki and Mbabane East.

    NowMbabane, Simunye, Bhunya, Piggs Peak and Mhlambanyatsi, with one branch manager each.

    Why: these are the branches that take applications.

Lending rules From LendMo’s answers

  1. Loans fall due on the client’s next payday

    Done

    BeforeRepayment was due one month after the money was paid out.

    NowRepayment is due on the client’s next payday after payout, using the pay date they gave, even if that is sooner than a month. The form shows the date (for example “Due 25 Nov 2026, your next payday”), and so does the loan agreement.

    Why: LendMo’s loans are payday loans.

  2. LendMo’s affordability rule

    Done

    BeforeA test rule: a safety buffer, and repayments no more than 40% of income.

    NowThe real net salary must cover the repayment plus one more interest charge, so E3,000 real net allows a loan of up to E2,000. Clients see “you can usually borrow up to…” and a warning if they ask for more. Staff see “Most they can borrow” when assessing. The Owner can change the rule in Lending settings.

    Why: this is how LendMo decides what a client can afford.

  3. Warning for more than 4 stop orders

    Done

    BeforeNothing flagged a client with many stop orders.

    NowStaff see a warning on the Loan & income and Screening steps when a client has more than 4 stop orders. Staff still make the decision.

    Why: LendMo says a client with more than 4 may not qualify.

  4. Applying again after a decline

    Done

    BeforeA declined client could apply again straight away for any amount.

    NowUntil their next payday, they can apply again only for a smaller amount. The form tells them the date and the limit. After payday, any amount is fine.

    Why: LendMo waits for the next payday to see whether net pay has improved.

  5. Consultants, and approvals up to E600 at branches

    Done

    BeforeThe “Loan officer” role could never approve loans.

    NowThe role is called Consultant. The Owner can let consultants approve loans, as with branch managers. Branch staff approve up to E600, and larger loans go to head office. A consultant who can approve sees their whole branch, but still can’t approve their own clients.

    Why: at LendMo, consultants and branch managers approve loans up to E600.

Demo version Test copy only

  1. One-click demo logins and a role switcher

    Done

    BeforeType an email and password for each role, and sign out to change role.

    NowButtons on the login page sign in as Borrower, Loan officer, Branch manager, Director, Owner or Admin in one click, and a switcher at the bottom changes role. Only in the test copy, never the live app.

    Why: makes it quick to show each role during a demo.

Speed and reliability Every screen

  1. Client home page no longer shows a setup error

    Done

    BeforeIn the test version, a client’s home page stayed on “Loading your account records…” and then showed “The company is not configured for borrower lending.”

    NowThe test version knows which company its clients belong to, so the home page and every other client screen load normally.

    Why: The test version wasn’t told which company clients sign up with unless a setting was typed in by hand each time.

  2. Pages load faster

    Done

    BeforePages asked the server for the same information several times at once, and the client home page made three separate requests. The first page opened could take around 10 seconds in the test version.

    NowEach page asks for each piece of information once, and the client home page asks once for everything. The test version gets the server ready in the background, so the first page doesn’t wait. Most pages open in under a second.

    Why: Loading was slow enough to look broken.

Smaller fixes

  • The contract end date is now saved when an application is saved to finish later.
  • Applications saved before this update are brought up to date automatically.
  • The Help assistant and the document checklist now ask for three months of payslips.
