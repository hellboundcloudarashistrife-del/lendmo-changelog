Cashbook Cash & Bank → Cashbook (new)

  1. A Cashbook page like Compuloan’s

    Done

    BeforeCash & Bank had a read-only list of checked payouts and repayments per branch. There was no list of the places money is kept and nothing could be done there.

    NowCash & Bank opens to two pages, Cashbook and Shift, as in Compuloan. Cashbook lists every branch cash box, bank account and mobile-money wallet with its branch and balance, totals for cash, bank and wallets, and Add and Refresh. Each row’s ⋮ menu has Update, Transactions and Deactivate.

    Why: LendMo asked for Compuloan’s Cash & Bank as it is, so moving across is easy.

  2. Balances and transactions for every cashbook

    Done

    BeforeBranch balances were typed in by hand and never changed with payouts or repayments.

    NowEach cashbook’s balance is its opening balance plus every confirmed payout, repayment, refund and wallet movement since. Transactions shows each one with the balance after it, the balance at the start and end of the dates chosen, a link to the loan, and Excel export. Directors set each opening balance, for example the Compuloan balance on the day of the move.

    Why: Staff need to see what each cash box, bank account and wallet actually holds.

  3. Bank payments are labelled as bank

    Done

    BeforeRepayments by bank transfer, debit order or Realpay showed as mobile money in the cashbook.

    NowThey show under the shared bank account cashbook as bank money.

    Why: The cashbook must say where the money really went.

Shift Cash & Bank → Shift

  1. Shifts open on a chosen cash box

    Done

    BeforeEvery shift used the branch’s one cash box, and the Open button vanished without saying why when you already had a shift open.

    NowOpen shift asks for the branch (Directors), the cash box and the float. Cash paid out or taken in during the shift goes to that cash box. If you already have a shift open, the page says which one and where.

    Why: Matches Compuloan, where each shift belongs to a cashbook.

  2. A ⋮ menu on every shift

    Done

    BeforeA shift could only be counted and closed; a closed shift couldn’t be looked at.

    NowEach shift’s ⋮ menu offers View (the opening count, every payout, repayment, refund and entry in it, and any counts that didn’t balance), Count and close, Print cash-up (a slip with signature lines) and Cancel shift for one opened by mistake while nothing is in it.

    Why: Staff need to check, print and correct shifts, as in Compuloan.

  3. Float top-ups, banking and expenses

    Done

    BeforeThere was nowhere to record cash brought into the cash box, cash taken to the bank, or petty-cash spending.

    NowDirectors and branch managers record them in their open shift: a float top-up (from head office or a bank account), cash banked into a chosen bank account, or an expense with its receipt number and what it was for. They count in the shift’s cash-up and move the cashbook balances at once. Directors are told about every expense.

    Why: Cash comes and goes for more than loans, and the cash-up must account for it.

  4. The old daily cash count is gone

    Done

    BeforeAn older “open the day / close the day” cash count still existed beside shifts.

    NowShifts are the only cash-up.

    Why: Two cash-ups could disagree about whether cash was confirmed.

Branch finance Branches → Finance and Setup, Capital Pool, Branch Manager overview

  1. Branch Finance shows the branch’s cashbooks

    Done

    BeforeThe Finance tab had hand-typed “Branch accounts” and a separate read-only cashbook list.

    NowIt shows the branch’s cashbooks with what each holds, the total and the lending budget, with links to Transactions and Add or Update for Directors. Accounts typed in before still appear, as cashbooks.

    Why: One set of figures for the branch’s money, everywhere.

  2. Capital Pool and branch setup use the cashbooks

    Done

    BeforeCapital Pool showed each branch’s hand-typed balance “as of” a date, and the setup guide asked for a branch account.

    NowCapital Pool shows what each branch’s cashbooks hold, the setup guide’s “Bank account and cash box” step adds a bank cashbook, and the Branch Manager’s cash reminder links to the cashbooks.

    Why: Balances should come from recorded money, not typing.

Director Settings → Lending settings, Employees, Settings → payout team, loan corrections

  1. The Director’s changes apply straight away

    Done

    BeforeChanging a payout wallet number, setting your own duties or approval limit, and deciding a correction you asked for all waited for “another Director”. LendMo has one Director, so they could never go through.

    NowA Director’s wallet changes apply straight away, and a change already waiting can be approved by the Director who asked. The Director can set their own duties and limit, and decide their own correction requests. Everything is still recorded in the history and audit trail. Checks between other staff, such as a second person confirming a payout or repayment, are unchanged.

    Why: LendMo has a single Director, the owner of the business.

Branches Branches list and Manage branch team

  1. All branches on one page

    Done

    BeforeThe Branches list showed 4 branches per page, so 6 branches needed two pages.

    NowAll branches show on one page. Page buttons only appear past 100 branches.

    Why: There is no reason to split such a short list.

  2. Readable team picker in dark mode

    Done

    BeforeIn Manage branch team, people who can’t be picked had a white background in dark mode, so their names were almost invisible.

    NowThose rows, the hover colour and the small text now follow light and dark mode, so every name is readable.

    Why: Reported in testing.

Buttons and messages Across the staff screens and the client portal

  1. Buttons that did the wrong thing are fixed

    Done

    BeforeSome buttons did something different from what they said: saving a note on a collection case changed its status to “Investigating”; “Update case” always failed on adjustment and write-off requests; the cash payout cancel form showed the mobile-money explanation; a loan officer saw a “Reject” button that could never be pressed; a client with a loan in progress was sent to their old application; and a notification about a case opened the whole list.

    NowA note keeps the case’s status. Adjustment and write-off requests link to the page where they are decided. The cash form explains cash. Loan officers see “Only a Director can decline an application.” The client is sent to their loan, and a notification opens that exact case. Help, Privacy and Terms on the password screen open their information instead of going back to log in.

    Why: Found in the button audit of 20 November.

  2. Branches can be paused and reopened

    Done

    BeforeThere was no way to pause a branch’s new applications, change who handles them, or reopen a deactivated branch from the branch menu.

    NowThe branch menu now has “Pause new applications”, “Change who handles new applications” and, for a paused or inactive branch, “Open for applications”.

    Why: The screens for this existed but no button opened them.

  3. Serious actions ask “are you sure?” first

    Done

    BeforeApproving, declining or sending back an application, confirming a payout, reversing a repayment, writing off a loan, cancelling a loan, closing a cash shift, saving a branch manager or team, and changing the payout team all happened on one click. A client accepting a loan online had no check at all.

    NowEach of these first shows a short question naming the client and the amount and saying what happens next, with a clear “Yes, …” button and a safe “Keep …” or “Not yet” button. The safe choice is the one selected.

    Why: These steps message the client, move money or can’t be undone, so a slip of the mouse shouldn’t be enough.

  4. Each money action says what happened

    Done

    BeforeEvery form on the Finance and loan screens said “Saved. The records below have been refreshed.”, whatever it did, and a refusal could appear out of sight further up the page.

    NowEach action now says what happened and who acts next, for example “Payout recorded. A second person now needs to check it before the loan starts.” The message also appears as a pop-up wherever you are, and a refusal is scrolled into view.

    Why: People couldn’t tell whether a button had worked.

  5. A way out of dead ends

    Done

    Before“Staff access required” only offered signing out. Home-page tiles could lead there. Some greyed-out buttons didn’t say why. A blocked print window quietly downloaded a file. On a slow phone connection a client could see “Unable to load your account”.

    Now“Staff access required” has a “Back to home” button, and home tiles only show pages you can open. Greyed-out buttons say what is missing (for example “To accept, tick the box above”). A blocked print window says a copy was downloaded instead. A slow connection now waits instead of failing.

    Why: People got stuck and didn’t know what to do next.

  6. Plain wording in error messages

    Done

    BeforeSome messages used technical wording, such as “This request ID was used for a different response”, “Journal does not balance” or a message about Firebase email delivery.

    NowThese now say what happened in plain words and what to do, for example “This form was already sent with different details. Refresh the page and enter it again.”

    Why: Messages should make sense to anyone using the app.

  7. No more browser pop-ups

    Done

    BeforeSaving staff duties, lending settings and a few other actions, and leaving a page with unsaved changes, used the browser’s grey “lendmo-loans-portal.web.app says” box with OK and Cancel.

    NowEvery question appears in the app’s own box with clear buttons, such as “Yes, save duties and limit” / “Go back” or “Yes, discard changes” / “Keep editing”.

    Why: The browser box looked like a warning from outside the app and its OK/Cancel didn’t say what would happen.

Wording Every screen, staff and client portal

  1. One name for each thing

    Done

    BeforeThe same thing had several names: the person borrowing was a client, customer, borrower or applicant; a repayment was also a receipt or collection; sending an application back was also “request information” and “info requested”; reminders were “dunning”, “due notices” and “in-app notices”.

    NowEach thing has one name everywhere: client, repayment, payout, send back to client, affordability check, payment reminder. Statuses read the same on every list, for the Director and for branch staff, and a client turning down an offer shows as “Client said no”, never “Rejected”.

    Why: Staff found the mixed names confusing.

  2. Buttons say what they do

    Done

    BeforeMany buttons used vague or misleading words, such as “Approve” (which also sends the offer), “Claim payout”, “Verify receipt”, “Post due interest and fees”, “Send setup email” (which sends a password reset), “Import client details” (which imports nothing) and five different “Save changes”.

    NowButtons say what happens and to what: “Approve and send offer”, “I’ll pay this out”, “Check repayment”, “Charge due interest and late fees”, “Email password link”, “Fill Add client from a spreadsheet”, “Save branch”, “Save payout team”. Messages after each button say what happened and who acts next.

    Why: So nobody has to guess what a button will do.

  3. Page names match the sidebar

    Done

    BeforeThe sidebar, the path at the top of the page and the page title often disagreed (for example “Process dunning”, “Process collection dunning”; “All loans & payouts”, “All loans”; “Employees”, “User management”).

    NowThey use the same name: for example “Send payment reminders”, “Payment reminder rules”, “Loans and payouts”, “Repayments”, “Declined and sent back”, “Employees”. Sidebar items stay in the same places. In the client portal, “Apply for a loan” opens “Apply for a loan”, the Help assistant gives the right answers for one-payment loans signed at the branch, and walk-in forms speak to staff (“How much does the client want?”).

    Why: People couldn’t tell whether they were on the page they clicked.

What to do next Staff home pages and loan pages

  1. A to-do list on every staff home page

    Done

    BeforeHome pages showed totals and charts, but not what needed doing. Staff had to know which page to open for each job.

    NowEach staff home page starts with a short to-do list for that person: clients waiting to sign, signed loans to pay out, payouts and repayments waiting for your check, applications to review, correction requests to decide, and overdue loans to call. Each line has one button that opens the right loan or application. People only see jobs their duties allow, and never a check on something they recorded themselves. When there is nothing to do it says “You’re up to date”.

    Why: Staff weren’t sure what to do or where to go.

  2. Each loan page shows where it is and the next step

    Done

    BeforeA loan page showed up to eight forms at once, with nothing saying which one was needed now.

    NowA progress line shows where the loan is (Offer, Signed, Paid out, Repaying, Paid off), and a highlighted box says the next step in one sentence, for example “SZL 2,500 overdue for 26 days. Call Zodwa Hlophe, then record what they pay.”, with the form for that step right there. Every other action is under “More actions”, closed until you open it.

    Why: So nobody has to work out which of many forms to use.

One look Every staff page and the client portal

  1. One button colour, readable text, one page header

    Done

    BeforeThe main button was gold on the sign-in page, red on the Overview, Clients and Collections, and blue on Finance and Capital Pool, so “Add branch” looked like a warning. Many labels were 9 to 11 pixels high. Pages used three different headers, and some printed their title twice.

    NowEvery main button is the logo gold; red is only for actions that remove, reverse or cancel. Links are blue. No text is smaller than 12 pixels. Every staff page has the same header (Home / section / page), using the same names as the sidebar, and the title appears once.

Tidier pages Finance, Customer support, Collections, Overview, Capital Pool, Clients, client portal

  1. Forms stay closed until you need them

    Done

    BeforeFinance was about 18 screens long, with two entry forms open above every accounting entry. Customer support had 12 forms open at once. Collection cases, corrections, write-offs, repayment notices and messages to clients all had a form open above their list.

    NowEach “create” form sits behind a button (for example “+ Open collection case”) and opens only when you press it. Finance shows accounting entries 25 at a time with a date filter. Customer support, collection cases and correction requests are short lists with a search box and Open / Waiting / Resolved / All filters; opening one shows just that one, with a link back. Pending My Approval shows “9 things to sort out before approval” on one line instead of nine bullet points. Opening a loan now shows that loan straight away, with a link back to the list.

  2. Calmer Overview, Capital Pool and Clients

    Done

    BeforeThe Director overview had 14 boxes, repeated figures, a “Target” column that always said “Not configured”, and large amounts that broke across lines. Capital Pool had three rows of tiles and boxes inside boxes. Clients had seven filters in a row.

    NowThe Overview has one row of totals, one row of money figures, Portfolio health, the chart (now labelled in SZL) and the branch table, after the to-do list. Capital Pool has one summary row and one table of branches. Clients shows search, branch and loan status, with the other filters under “More filters”, and an unfinished client shows as a small notice.

    Why: Too much on screen at once.

  3. Client portal: the right thing first

    Done

    BeforeA client with a loan already open saw the whole application form, which they couldn’t send. On a phone, the first question was more than a screen down. The home page repeated the same amount in three boxes.

    NowA client with a loan open sees only a message and a “View your loan” button. On a phone, the steps and the first question come first, the document list folds away, and the step tabs are one compact row. The home page starts with one “What you owe” card: the next payment, when it’s due, and “How to pay”.

    Why: Most clients use a phone.

Reminders and backups Behind the scenes

  1. Reminders reach walk-in clients, and say how much and when

    Ready, not live yet

    BeforeClients who signed at a branch without an online account got no reminders, notices or statements at all. Emails only said “There is an update in your account”. A notice of default sent by email was cancelled before it went out. If the reminder job missed a day, that reminder was lost.

    NowWalk-in clients are emailed at the address they chose for documents when they signed (or their application email); those without an email stay on the staff to-do list to phone. Emails carry the actual message, for example “SZL 1,875.00 is due on 25 Nov 2026 for loan 414F9EA0. Pay by MoMo…”. A notice of default always goes by email. A missed day is caught up on the next run. This goes live once LendMo’s email account is set up and the jobs are released.

    Why: Almost every LendMo client is a walk-in, and they weren’t being reminded.

  2. Nightly backup of the database

    Ready, not live yet

    BeforeThere was no backup of the live database.

    NowA nightly job copies the whole database to a separate storage area at 2am, in a folder per night, with written steps for restoring it. It starts once the storage area is created and the jobs are released.

    Why: So a mistake or a fault can be undone.

Money handling Loans and payouts, Repayments, Finance, Cash & Bank

  1. Payments on a written-off loan

    Done

    BeforeMoney a client paid after their loan was written off couldn’t be recorded against the loan.

    NowStaff record it as usual and a second person checks it. It is booked as money recovered; the loan stays written off and shows “Recovered so far”.

    Why: Clients sometimes pay after a write-off.

  2. Reverse any repayment, not just the newest

    Done

    BeforeTo reverse an older repayment (for example one that bounced), every later repayment had to be reversed and entered again, each needing two people.

    NowAny confirmed repayment can be reversed in one step. The later repayments are re-applied to the loan automatically, and the question before it says how many.

    Why: Saves a long, error-prone sequence.

  3. Refunds by cash or bank transfer

    Done

    BeforeRefunds of extra money a client paid could only be sent by mobile money.

    NowRefunds can also be paid in cash from the branch cash box (counted in the cash shift and confirmed when it closes) or by bank transfer.

    Why: Clients repay by cash and bank too.

  4. One transfer paying several loans

    Done

    BeforeA single bank transfer or debit-order batch covering several clients could only be recorded once under its reference.

    NowTick “This reference covers several loans” and enter the transfer total; each loan’s share is recorded under the real reference, up to that total.

    Why: So records match the bank statement.

  5. Attach the slip

    Done

    BeforePayouts, repayments, refunds, reversals and wallet movements only took a typed reference.

    NowA photo or PDF of the slip or statement can be attached, and opened later with “View slip”. Every view is recorded.

    Why: Proof stays with the record.

  6. See who can check it, and tell them

    Done

    BeforeWhen a payout or repayment waited for a second person, nothing said who could check it, and nobody was told.

    NowEach waiting item says who can check it, with a “Tell them” button that sends them a message. If nobody else has the duty, it says to ask a Director to give someone that duty. The loan’s next step now mentions a repayment waiting for a check before chasing the client.

    Why: Work was getting stuck waiting on a second person.

Staff tools Clients, Reports, every list, Cash & Bank, Audit trail, Settings

  1. Import many clients from a spreadsheet

    Done

    BeforeImporting a spreadsheet only filled in Add client one row at a time.

    NowCheck the file, see how many rows are ready, need fixing or already exist, then add all ready rows at once (client records only, no loans). Rows that already exist are skipped, never overwritten, and skipped rows can be downloaded with the reason.

    Why: To move LendMo’s existing client book in.

  2. Two new reports

    Done

    BeforeThere were four reports, none on write-offs or income.

    Now“Write-offs and Recoveries” lists each written-off loan with the amount, what has been recovered since and what is still unrecovered. “Interest and Fee Income” shows interest and late fees earned per month and branch, with waivers and recoveries shown separately. Both are for Directors with the finance duty.

    Why: Asked for by the business.

  3. Choose how many rows to show

    Done

    BeforeMost lists showed 10 rows; Pending My Approval showed 4; the overview showed only the first 4 approved loans.

    NowEvery list lets you choose 25, 50 or 100 rows, and remembers your choice.

    Why: Too much paging through tiny lists.

  4. Longer cash history, exports and audit search

    Done

    BeforeThe cashbook showed only the last 30 days, cash counts 7 days and shifts the latest 300. The audit trail had no search, and several lists couldn’t be exported.

    NowCashbook, cash counts and cash shifts have a From and To date. Cash shifts, cashbook, Employees, Branches, the audit trail and the Capital Pool table can be exported to Excel. The audit trail can be searched by date, person and action.

    Why: Needed for month-end checks.

  5. Change your own password

    Done

    BeforeAccount & security in Settings said “Coming later”.

    NowStaff can change their password there (current password, new password, confirm), or ask for a reset email.

    Why: Basic account safety.

Client portal Apply for a loan, My loans, statements and receipts

  1. The application form saves itself

    Done

    BeforeThe form only saved when you pressed Save. Refreshing the page or losing signal lost what you had typed and sent you back to step 1, and documents only uploaded when you saved.

    NowThe form saves a few seconds after you stop typing (“Saved 10:42”), keeps a backup on the phone if the signal drops, and reopens on the step you were on. A document starts uploading as soon as you choose it. Staff walk-in forms save the same way.

    Why: Most clients apply on a phone with a patchy signal.

  2. Several files for one document

    Done

    BeforeEach document took one file, so a two-sided ID card or a bank statement in three files didn’t fit.

    NowEach document takes up to 5 files (“Add the front and back” for the ID card), each with View and Remove. Staff see and check all of them together.

    Why: Real documents often come in several pages.

  3. PDF statements, receipts and quotes

    Done

    BeforeStatements, receipts, repayment notices and quotes downloaded as web pages, which are awkward to open on a phone. The loan planner talked about monthly repayments.

    NowThey download as proper PDF files with the LendMo logo and the same figures. The planner says “One payment on your next payday” for LendMo’s one-payment loans.

    Why: Clients and staff need documents they can keep and send.

Arrears interest and speed Overdue loans, Lending settings, every loan list

  1. Arrears interest on overdue loans

    Done

    BeforeA loan that stayed overdue only ever got the one late fee.

    NowFor each full month a loan stays overdue, 25% of what is overdue is added as arrears interest (simple, never on earlier arrears interest). The total never goes above the principal still unpaid. It is paused while a payment is being checked, during a dispute, a valid settlement figure or a cooling-off cancellation. The client is told by email, and it shows on the loan page, the client’s home page, statements and the Interest and Fee Income report. The rate and on/off switch are in Lending settings.

    Why: LendMo asked for “25% of what’s owed, per full month”.

  2. Arrears interest can be corrected

    Done

    BeforeFix a charge covered interest and late fees only.

    NowFix a charge can also reduce posted arrears interest, and each new loan keeps the arrears rule it was offered with.

    Why: Mistakes must be fixable, and a later settings change mustn’t change signed loans.

  3. Faster pages as the loan book grows

    Done

    BeforeLoan pages, home to-do lists, risk checks and shift close read every record, one loan at a time.

    NowThey read only what they need: with 24 loans, the loans page went from 73 lookups to 4; risk checks read about a quarter of the records; shift close reads only loans holding cash. What you see is unchanged.

    Why: Pages would slow down badly as LendMo lends more.

    BeforeOn a staff member’s page, the tick boxes for their duties were stretched across the whole column. Their labels were pushed off the edge, the page scrolled sideways, and the rows were spaced unevenly.

    NowThe tick boxes are normal size and the duties line up in two neat columns (one column on a phone). Nothing runs off the page.

    Why: the settings didn’t fit or line up, even though there isn’t much on the page.

  4. Screening checks explain themselves

    Done

    BeforeA list of checks marked “Pending · Not recorded”, with no way to tell which were needed, what each meant, or how to complete them.

    NowEach check says what staff should do (for example, Contact: call the applicant to confirm their number) and whether it’s required for approval or optional. Each one has its own “Record this check” button.

    Why: staff got stuck on the screening step.

  5. Wallet check no longer needs a file

    Done

    BeforeThe wallet ownership check couldn’t be passed without a wallet ownership file, even though customers are told that file is optional.

    NowStaff can pass the wallet check by recording how they confirmed the registered name (for example, a MoMo name lookup). A file can still be attached if there is one.

    Why: applications without the optional file could never be approved.

  6. Recording a check is quicker and harder to get wrong

    Done

    BeforeOne long form at the bottom of the page with seven boxes, including two date-and-time pickers and a reference number. The result started on “Pending”, so a check could be saved as not passed without anyone noticing.

    Now“Record this check” opens a small window for that check. You pick Passed, Failed or Couldn’t check yet (nothing is picked for you), choose how you checked from a list, and write what you found. The time is filled in and the check lasts as long as the company setting says. A reference number is only needed for a credit bureau check.

    Why: an ID check was saved as “Pending” by mistake.

  7. Screening shows what to do, in order

    Done

    BeforeNothing said that documents must be verified before the checks, or that the affordability assessment has to be run last.

    NowThree numbered steps sit at the top: verify the documents, record the required checks (for example “1 of 3 passed”), then run the affordability assessment. Each step gets a tick when it’s done. If a check needs a document that hasn’t been verified yet, the window says so and has a button that opens Documents.

    Why: the ID check couldn’t pass because the ID card hadn’t been verified, and the screen didn’t say so.

  8. Reviewing a document no longer wipes other checks

    Done

    BeforeMarking any document as verified or rejected erased every check already recorded on the application.

    NowOnly checks that used that document are cleared. For example, rejecting the ID card clears the Identity check, but the Contact check stays.

    Why: finished checks were disappearing.

  9. All three payslips count as evidence

    Done

    BeforeOnly the month 1 payslip could back up the employment check and the affordability assessment.

    NowAny of the three payslips can be used.

    Why: the form now collects three payslips.

Lending rules From LendMo’s answers

  1. Loans fall due on the client’s next payday

    Done

    BeforeRepayment was due one month after the money was paid out.

    NowRepayment is due on the client’s next payday after payout, using the pay date they gave, even if that is sooner than a month. The form shows the date (for example “Due 25 Nov 2026, your next payday”), and so does the loan agreement.

    Why: LendMo’s loans are payday loans.

  2. LendMo’s affordability rule

    Done

    BeforeA test rule: a safety buffer, and repayments no more than 40% of income.

    NowThe real net salary must cover the repayment plus one more interest charge, so E3,000 real net allows a loan of up to E2,000. Clients see “you can usually borrow up to…” and a warning if they ask for more. Staff see “Most they can borrow” when assessing. The Owner can change the rule in Lending settings.

    Why: this is how LendMo decides what a client can afford.

  3. Warning for more than 4 stop orders

    Done

    BeforeNothing flagged a client with many stop orders.

    NowStaff see a warning on the Loan & income and Screening steps when a client has more than 4 stop orders. Staff still make the decision.

    Why: LendMo says a client with more than 4 may not qualify.

  4. Applying again after a decline

    Done

    BeforeA declined client could apply again straight away for any amount.

    NowUntil their next payday, they can apply again only for a smaller amount. The form tells them the date and the limit. After payday, any amount is fine.

    Why: LendMo waits for the next payday to see whether net pay has improved.

  5. Consultants, and approvals up to E600 at branches

    Done

    BeforeThe “Loan officer” role could never approve loans.

    NowThe role is called Consultant. The Owner can let consultants approve loans, as with branch managers. Branch staff approve up to E600, and larger loans go to head office. A consultant who can approve sees their whole branch, but still can’t approve their own clients.

    Why: at LendMo, consultants and branch managers approve loans up to E600.
